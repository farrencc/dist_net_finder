WHAT IT SHOWS
  A 3x2 confusion matrix drawn as a Blues heatmap with the counts written into
  each cell and a colourbar from 0 to 60. Rows are the planted blocks -
  "block 0", "block 1", "border" - and columns are the two communities Louvain
  returned.

  Block 0 maps entirely to community 0 (60 and 0). Block 1 maps entirely to
  community 1 (0 and 60). The 20-node border block splits exactly down the
  middle, 10 to each side, and shows as a pale row.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Stochastic block model: SIZES = [60, 60, 20], P_IN = 0.20, P_CROSS = 0.004,
  P_BORDER_OUT = 0.12, P_BORDER_IN = 0.02, seed = 7.

WHY IT IS HERE
  Two messages in one picture.

  The good news: on structure that genuinely exists, Louvain recovers it
  perfectly - 120 of 120 nodes in the two main blocks, no errors.

  The honest news: the border block was built to be ambiguous. It is
  deliberately sparser inside (P_BORDER_IN = 0.02) than the two big blocks
  (P_IN = 0.20) and reasonably well connected to both, so it is not really a
  community at all. The even 10/10 split is not a failure - there is no correct
  answer to give, and any method would have to invent one. Use this to warn
  against reading every returned community as a real thing.
