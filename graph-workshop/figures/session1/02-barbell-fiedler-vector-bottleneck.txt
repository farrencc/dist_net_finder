TITLE
  Fiedler vector of a barbell graph: the gap is the cut

SOURCE
  Notebook : graph-workshop/notebooks/session1_instructor.ipynb
  Cell     : 24
  Section  : 3 - Laplacian (clock 0:20)
  Image    : 02-barbell-fiedler-vector-bottleneck.png

WHAT IT SHOWS
  Two panels.

  Left: a barbell(8, 0) graph - two 8-node cliques joined by a single edge -
  drawn with a spring layout. Nodes are coloured by their entry in the Fiedler
  vector on a blue-white-red (coolwarm) scale with a colourbar labelled
  "Fiedler entry". One clique is uniformly deep blue at about -0.25, the other
  uniformly deep red at about +0.25, and the two bridge nodes sit slightly
  toward the middle of the scale.

  Right: the same Fiedler entries sorted ascending and plotted as a line with
  markers, titled "the gap is the cut". The curve is flat at about -0.26 for
  nodes 0-6, jumps almost vertically through zero between sorted positions 7
  and 9, then is flat again at about +0.26 for the rest.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  barbell(8, 0), lambda_2 = 0.2042
  16 nodes, two cliques of 8, one bridge edge.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The single most important picture in Session 1. The Fiedler vector - the
  eigenvector of the second-smallest Laplacian eigenvalue - assigns each node a
  number, and the sign of that number partitions the graph exactly at its
  bottleneck. Nobody told the algorithm where the bridge was.

  The right panel is the honest diagnostic: a plateau, a cliff, a plateau means
  the split is unambiguous. When that curve is a smooth ramp instead, the graph
  has no natural two-way cut and the sign split is an arbitrary decision. Read
  this shape before trusting any spectral partition.
