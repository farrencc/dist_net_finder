TITLE
  The same moons in embedded coordinates collapse to two points

SOURCE
  Notebook : graph-workshop/notebooks/session1_instructor.ipynb
  Cell     : 34
  Section  : 4 - Spectral clustering (clock 0:50)
  Image    : 05-spectral-embedding-two-moons.png

WHAT IT SHOWS
  Two panels.

  Left, "original coordinates": the two-moons data coloured by the labels from
  a hand-built spectral pipeline - a 12-nearest-neighbour graph, its Laplacian,
  the first two non-trivial eigenvectors, then k-means in that space. The
  colours now follow the crescents correctly, red for the upper moon and blue
  for the lower.

  Right, "embedded coordinates: two points": the same 400 points plotted with
  eigenvector 1 on the x axis and eigenvector 2 on the y axis. They no longer
  look like moons at all - they collapse onto two tight, nearly collinear
  streaks with a clear empty gap between them, red at the lower left and blue
  at the upper right.

  Note the axis offsets on the right panel (about 1e-14 and -1.31e-2): the
  spread within each streak is numerically tiny compared with the gap between
  them.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  K_NN = 12 nearest neighbours, symmetrised before use.
  k = 2 eigenvectors used for the embedding.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The mechanism, in one image. Spectral clustering does not cluster the data;
  it clusters an embedding of the data, and k-means at the end is only there to
  finish an already-trivial job.

  The geometry that made the moons hard - non-convex, interlocking - is gone
  after the change of coordinates. Pair this directly with the k-means failure
  figure: same data, same final algorithm, different coordinates, opposite
  outcome.
