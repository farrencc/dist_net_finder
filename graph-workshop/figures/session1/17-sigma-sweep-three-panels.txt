TITLE
  Sigma sweep: the same computation, three answers

SOURCE
  Notebook : graph-workshop/notebooks/session1_instructor.ipynb
  Cell     : 59
  Section  : 7 - Applied: weighted clustering (clock 2:05)
  Image    : 17-sigma-sweep-three-panels.png

WHAT IT SHOWS
  Three portrait maps in a row, all coolwarm, under the figure title
  "large sigma: weights near 1, pure geography.   small sigma: every edge
  cut."

  Left, sigma = 4.28 (10x the median): a smooth blue-to-red gradient from the
  north-east to the south-west - visibly the same picture as the unweighted,
  geometry-only Fiedler map. The attribute has been weighted out of existence.

  Centre, sigma = 0.43 (the median): the balanced result - red north, neutral
  south-central band, deep blue western pocket.

  Right, sigma = 0.17 (0.4x the median): almost the entire map saturates to a
  single red, with one small blue pocket left in the west. The contrast has
  collapsed.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  sigma = 4.28, lambda_2 = 1.1e-02
  sigma = 0.43, lambda_2 = 5.4e-03
  sigma = 0.17, lambda_2 = 2.6e-06   <- six orders of magnitude down

  0.4x rather than 0.1x is deliberate: below about 0.3x the median, this graph
  separates into components outright and lambda_2 goes to zero.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The methodological warning of Session 1, and the best slide in the deck for
  making it stick. Sigma is not a detail, it is the model.

  Too large and every weight approaches 1, the attribute is ignored and you have
  computed pure geography with extra steps. Too small and nearly every edge is
  driven to zero, the weighted graph falls apart, and lambda_2 collapses toward
  zero - at which point the "second eigenvector" is an essentially arbitrary
  vector drawn from a near-null space. The right-hand map is not a result. It is
  noise that happens to be plausible-looking, which is far more dangerous than
  noise that looks like noise.

  The defence is the printed number, not the picture: watch lambda_2, and
  disbelieve any map whose lambda_2 has collapsed.
