  Three ways to draw one line through Dublin

  Image    : 18-unweighted-weighted-kmeans-cluster-comparison.png

WHAT IT SHOWS
  Three categorical maps in a row, each a two-colour split (Set2: grey and
  teal), no axes.

  Left, "unweighted Fiedler": teal covers the north and north-east, grey the
  south and west. A clean, contiguous, geographically tidy division that owes
  nothing to the census data.

  Centre, "weighted, sigma = 0.43": teal now covers the southern band plus the
  western pocket, grey the north. Still fully contiguous, but the line has moved
  to follow the attribute.

  Right, "k-means on the attribute, no graph": teal appears as scattered patches
  all over the map with no spatial coherence at all - neighbouring EDs routinely
  land in different clusters.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Labels are the sign of the respective Fiedler vectors; the k-means panel is
  KMeans(n_clusters=2) run on the standardised attribute alone, ignoring the
  graph entirely.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The summary slide for Session 1. Three methods, one dataset, three genuinely
  different notions of what a region is.

  Geometry alone gives you contiguity without meaning. The attribute alone gives
  you meaning without contiguity - the right-hand map is not a set of regions,
  it is a recolouring. Only the weighted graph delivers both at once, and that
  is the entire argument for doing any of this.

  The centre map is also the object Session 2 spends its capstone testing.
