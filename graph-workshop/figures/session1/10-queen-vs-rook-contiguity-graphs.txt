WHAT IT SHOWS
  Two panels, each showing the Dublin EDs as pale grey polygons with the
  contiguity graph drawn over them in crimson - one line segment per adjacency,
  joining the representative points of neighbouring EDs.

  Left, Queen contiguity: two EDs are neighbours if they share any boundary
  point, corners included.
  Right, Rook contiguity: they must share an actual boundary segment.

  The two webs look broadly alike; the difference is in the extra corner-only
  links that the Queen panel carries, most visible where several EDs meet at a
  point.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Queen: 462 edges, mean degree 5.70
  Rook:  414 edges, mean degree 5.11
  A 48-edge difference on the same 162 polygons.

WHY IT IS HERE
  There is no canonical graph hiding inside a map - you build one, and the rule
  you pick changes the answer. Everything downstream (the Laplacian, the Fiedler
  vector, betweenness, Moran's I, the diffusion) runs on whichever of these two
  you chose.

  The workshop uses Queen from here on. The point is not that Queen is right,
  it is that the choice was made deliberately and should be stated.
