WHAT IT SHOWS
  An OSMnx street-network plot on a white background: thin dark grey lines for
  drivable road segments and small crimson dots at every junction, covering the
  Rathmines and Ranelagh area of south Dublin. Title: "Rathmines / Ranelagh,
  drive network".

  The texture is immediately different from the contiguity graphs - long
  through-routes, dense terraced grids in the north-west, and many short
  dead-end stubs.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  414 nodes, 909 edges.
  Loaded from graph-workshop/data/streets.graphml.

WHY IT IS HERE
  A 90-second cameo making the point that contiguity is not the only way a map
  becomes a graph. Here nodes are junctions and edges are road segments, and the
  same Laplacian, the same eigenvectors and the same centrality measures all
  apply unchanged.

  The cell is guarded by a HAVE_OSMNX flag and prints a skip message if the
  import failed, so in a live run this figure may be absent. Treat it as
  optional in the deck too.
