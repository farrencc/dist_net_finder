TITLE
  Three spectral clusterings of two moons: the affinity is the model

SOURCE
  Notebook : graph-workshop/notebooks/session1_instructor.ipynb
  Cell     : 35
  Section  : 4 - Spectral clustering (clock 0:50)
  Image    : 06-spectral-clustering-affinity-comparison.png

WHAT IT SHOWS
  Three scatter panels of the same two-moons data, sharing axes.

  Panel 1, "by hand": the hand-built 12-NN pipeline. The split follows the two
  crescents.

  Panel 2, 'affinity="nearest_neighbors"': sklearn's SpectralClustering with a
  kNN affinity. Same partition as panel 1, with the two colours swapped -
  cluster labels are arbitrary, the partition is what matters.

  Panel 3, 'affinity="rbf"  (the default)': the boundary no longer follows the
  crescents. It cuts across them, and the left tip of the lower moon is grouped
  with the upper moon instead of with the rest of its own crescent.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  n_neighbors = 12 for the kNN affinity; the rbf panel uses sklearn's default
  gamma. random_state = 7 throughout.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The graph you build is the modelling decision, and it is made before any
  eigenvector is computed. A kNN affinity only connects points to their local
  neighbours, so it follows the shape of the manifold. A global RBF kernel at a
  default bandwidth connects everything to everything with a smoothly decaying
  weight, which quietly reintroduces the straight-line geometry that spectral
  clustering was supposed to escape.

  Practical warning to deliver with it: rbf is the default, so this is the
  behaviour you get if you never pass an affinity argument.
