WHAT IT SHOWS
  Two scatter panels sharing axes, 400 points from sklearn's make_moons with
  noise 0.10 - two interlocking crescents.

  Left, "two moons": points coloured by their true label, blue for the upper
  crescent, red for the lower one.

  Right, "k-means: a straight line, and it cannot help it": the same points
  coloured by a 2-cluster k-means fit. The boundary is a near-vertical straight
  line around x = 0.5 that slices both crescents in half, so each cluster is
  half of one moon and half of the other.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  400 points, noise = 0.10, random_state = 7.
  KMeans(n_clusters=2, n_init=10).

WHY IT IS HERE
  Sets up the problem spectral clustering exists to solve. k-means assigns each
  point to the nearest centroid, so its decision boundaries are always straight
  - the cells of a Voronoi diagram. Interlocking, non-convex shapes are not
  merely hard for it, they are outside what it can express.

  The phrase in the panel title matters: "and it cannot help it". This is not a
  tuning failure or a bad initialisation, it is the model class.
