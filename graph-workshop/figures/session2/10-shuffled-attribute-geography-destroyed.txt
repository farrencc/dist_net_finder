  Control: shuffle the values and the structure disappears

SOURCE
  Notebook : graph-workshop/notebooks/session2_instructor.ipynb

WHAT IT SHOWS
  A 2x2 grid of maps.

  Top-left, "attribute": the real third-level education values on viridis -
  smooth, spatially coherent, high in the south.
  Top-right, "attribute, shuffled": the identical set of values randomly
  reassigned across EDs. Salt-and-pepper, no spatial structure whatsoever.
  Bottom-left, "weighted clusters" (Set2 teal/grey): the real weighted Fiedler
  split - a coherent southern teal region against a northern grey one.
  Bottom-right, "weighted clusters, shuffled": the same pipeline on the shuffled
  values. It still returns two clusters, but teal now sprawls across most of the
  map with grey confined to the north-east - a lopsided split with no
  interpretable boundary.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Moran's I   before +0.7176 (p = 0.001)   after -0.0392 (p = 0.248)
  cluster sizes before [66, 96]           after [128, 34]

  Same values, same map, same sigma - only the pairing between value and place
  was permuted.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The control experiment, and the most transferable idea in either session.

  Destroying the geography does exactly what it should to the statistic: Moran's
  I collapses from +0.72 to -0.04 and stops being significant. The test knows
  the structure is gone.

  The clustering does not know. It returns two clusters, because that is what it
  was asked for and it will always return two clusters - on real data, on
  shuffled data, on noise. The only signals that anything is wrong are indirect:
  the split is badly unbalanced at [128, 34] where the real one was [66, 96],
  and the resulting regions answer to nothing.

  The general lesson to close on: an algorithm that always produces an answer
  tells you nothing by producing one. The null is what makes the answer mean
  something, which is why the capstone was built around one.
