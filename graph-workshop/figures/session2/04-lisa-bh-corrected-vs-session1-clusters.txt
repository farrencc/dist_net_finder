WHAT IT SHOWS
  Two panels.

  Left, "LISA, BH-corrected": the same local Moran's I results after a
  Benjamini-Hochberg correction, drawn categorically on Set1 - HH red, LH
  purple, LL brown, not significant grey. Far fewer EDs survive than in the
  uncorrected map: a compact red HH region in the south, scattered brown LL
  patches across the north and north-west and one in the far west, and a thin
  purple LH strip along the docklands. The grey not-significant majority has
  grown noticeably.

  Right, "Session 1 weighted clusters, sigma = 0.43": the Session 1 weighted
  Fiedler split, teal for the southern region and grey for the north.

  The red HH region on the left and the teal region on the right cover
  substantially the same ground.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Benjamini-Hochberg applied across all 162 local tests at alpha = 0.05.
  Compare directly against the uncorrected p < 0.05 map.

WHY IT IS HERE
  Two points, deliberately placed side by side.

  First, multiple comparisons. 162 local tests at alpha = 0.05 will hand you
  roughly eight false positives even on pure noise. Benjamini-Hochberg controls
  the false discovery rate, and the visible shrinkage against the previous
  figure is what that correction costs - and what the uncorrected map was
  quietly overstating.

  Second, convergence. Two methods with almost nothing in common - a
  permutation test on local spatial autocorrelation, and an eigenvector of a
  similarity-weighted graph Laplacian - independently identify the same division
  of the city. Neither validates the other formally, but agreement between
  unrelated methods is real evidence, and it is the reason the capstone treats
  the Session 1 boundary as worth testing.
