TITLE
  The Moran scatterplot: I is a regression slope

SOURCE
  Notebook : graph-workshop/notebooks/session2_instructor.ipynb
  Cell     : 12
  Section  : 2 - Moran's I (clock 0:10) - optional, marked cut-first
  Image    : 02-moran-scatterplot.png

WHAT IT SHOWS
  A near-square scatter with the standardised attribute z on the x axis and its
  spatial lag - the row-standardised average of z over each ED's neighbours - on
  the y axis. Points are pale grey. Dashed grey lines at x = 0 and y = 0 divide
  the plane into four quadrants, and a salmon regression line runs through the
  origin. A crimson annotation reads "slope = I = 0.718".

  The cloud is clearly elongated along the fitted line, with most points in the
  upper-right and lower-left quadrants.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Regression slope = Moran's I = 0.718.
  Quadrants: upper-right HH, lower-left LL, upper-left LH, lower-right HL.

WHY IT IS HERE
  Demystifies the formula. Moran's I is not an exotic spatial statistic - it is
  the slope of a plain regression of a place's neighbourhood average on the
  place's own value. Positive slope means high values sit near high values.

  The quadrants are load-bearing for the next figure: they are exactly the four
  LISA categories. A point far out along the upper-right diagonal is an HH
  cluster member; a point stranded in the lower-right is a high value marooned
  among low neighbours.

  The notebook marks this cut-first - the permutation histogram has already made
  the argument.
