  The heat front stalls exactly at the Fiedler boundary

SOURCE

WHAT IT SHOWS
  A single portrait map: the t = 1/lambda_2 diffusion snapshot on inferno, with
  the boundary of the positive-Fiedler region dissolved into one polygon and
  traced over it as a thick cyan line. The source ED is outlined in white just
  north of the river.

  The cyan curve wraps the southern and south-western part of the city. The
  colour transition in the underlying heat field - bright yellows and oranges to
  the north-east, deep purples and black to the south-west - runs essentially
  along that same line.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  Snapshot at t = 1.0 * tau, tau = 1/lambda_2.
  Cyan line: boundary of the region where the Fiedler entry is positive.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The payoff of the diffusion section, and the visual argument the capstone then
  measures numerically.

  Two apparently unrelated computations - partitioning a graph by the sign of an
  eigenvector, and simulating heat spreading through it - produce the same line.
  They are the same fact seen from two directions: the slowest-decaying mode
  governs the late-time shape of the diffusion, and that mode is the Fiedler
  vector.

  Stated more usefully: the spectral boundary is not a label placed on the map
  from outside. It is where things actually have difficulty crossing.

  Marked cut-first in the notebook; the capstone does not require it.
