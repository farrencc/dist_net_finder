WHAT IT SHOWS
  A single grey histogram with white bar edges, titled "999 permutations of the
  same values over the same map". The x axis is Moran's I under random
  reassignment, the y axis counts permutations.

  The null distribution is a tidy bell centred just below zero, spanning roughly
  -0.15 to +0.15. A black dashed vertical line marks E[I] = -0.0062. A thick
  crimson vertical line marks the observed I = +0.7176, sitting far off to the
  right with an enormous empty gap between it and the nearest null draw.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  I       = +0.7176
  E[I]    = -0.0062  (= -1/(n-1), not zero)
  z-score = large and positive
  p_sim   = 0.0010   (999 permutations - the smallest value this test can report)

WHY IT IS HERE
  How inference works when the sampling distribution is unknown. Rather than
  assume a distribution, keep the map and the values exactly as they are and
  break only the pairing between them, 999 times. That yields the distribution
  of I under "no spatial relationship", against which the real value is
  compared.

  The gap here is not marginal - the observed value is nowhere near the null, so
  the honest report is "p is at the floor of what 999 permutations can resolve",
  not "p = 0.001 exactly".

  The small detail worth stating: E[I] is -1/(n-1), not zero. Under the null,
  Moran's I is very slightly negative by construction.
