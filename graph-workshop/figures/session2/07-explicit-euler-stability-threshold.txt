WHAT IT SHOWS
  A semi-log plot, Euler step number 0-400 on the x axis and max_i |u_i| on a
  logarithmic y axis.

  The blue solid curve, at dt = 0.90 x 2/lambda_max, drops quickly and then
  settles into a slow, smooth decay toward about 6e-3 - correct behaviour for a
  diffusion that is spreading mass out.

  The orange dashed curve, at dt = 1.01 x 2/lambda_max, tracks the blue one for
  about 25 steps, then turns around and grows steadily - with a visible
  sawtooth oscillation - reaching about 2e1 by step 400. On a log axis that
  straight upward line is exponential blow-up.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  lambda_max     = 12.626
  2 x max degree = 22        (the bound lambda_max cannot exceed)
  stable dt      < 0.1584    (= 2/lambda_max)

  The two runs differ by 1%: 0.90x versus 1.01x the threshold.

WHY IT IS HERE
  A short, sharp numerical-methods warning. Explicit Euler on du/dt = -Lu is
  stable only when dt < 2/lambda_max, and the failure at 1% over the line is not
  a gentle loss of accuracy - it is exponential divergence, with the
  step-to-step oscillation that identifies this specific instability.

  The practical takeaway is the cheap bound: lambda_max can never exceed twice
  the maximum degree, so a safe step size is available from the graph without
  computing a single eigenvalue.
