WHAT IT SHOWS
  Two histogram panels sharing a y axis. The x axis is "mass on the far side"
  - how much of the diffused unit mass has crossed the boundary by
  t = 1/lambda_2 - and the y axis counts draws from the null.

  Each panel carries two nulls. The pale grey histogram is the scattered-labels
  null: tight and roughly normal. The dark grey histogram is the
  contiguous-regions null: much broader, left-skewed, with a long right tail. A
  thick crimson vertical line marks the observed value.

  Left, "weighted boundary (rank 0 of 1999)": observed 0.3235, sitting at the
  extreme left edge, below every one of the 1999 contiguous draws.
  Right, "unweighted boundary (rank 0 of 1999)": observed about 0.487, again
  clear of everything in both nulls.

NUMBERS ON THE FIGURE
  DRAWS = 1999 for each null.
  Weighted boundary   : observed 0.3235, rank 0 of 1999
  Unweighted boundary : observed ~0.487, rank 0 of 1999
  Rank 0 gives p = 1/2000 = 0.0005 - the smallest value this null can report.

WHY IT IS HERE
  The worked capstone, and the methodological heart of Session 2.

  The measurement: release unit mass at the highest-betweenness ED, diffuse to
  t = 1/lambda_2, and sum whatever ended up on the far side of the Session 1
  boundary. Less mass crossing means the boundary held more back.

  The nulls are the real lesson, and the two are deliberately different in
  quality. Scattering the labels randomly produces no boundary at all, so almost
  any real division beats it - it is far too easy, which is precisely why it is
  shown. Growing random contiguous regions of the same size is the honest
  comparison: a genuine boundary, but an arbitrary one.

  Both Session 1 boundaries beat all 1999 contiguous draws. Rank 0 of 1999 is a
  clear win, and note that the weighted boundary holds back more (0.32 crossing)
  than the geometry-only one (0.49) - the attribute weighting bought real
  containment, not just a prettier map.

  Also worth saying: p = 0.0005 is the floor of this test, not a precise
  estimate. The correct claim is "better than every draw we took".
